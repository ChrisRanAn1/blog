I started on May 15th 2026. I decided to start with rust and Solana because I heard it is harder, and it's also my favorite blockchain. As a witness in the last cycle of the prosperity of solana and meme coins, I personally participated at a very early stage, made many x on the meme coin $BOME, and some other infrastructure like Jupiter and Radium which makes me have a unique emotion of Solana because I didn't make any profit on other chains. It is very interesting to see the industry in a totally different perspective and dive deeper.

I spent my entire first month on rust, starting with The Rust Book, because I don't have too much coding experience on other programming languages, I didn't feel the pain of learning rust at the start. I also practice coding with the Rare code.

After that I dive into solana. I learnt with the rare skill course 60 days Solana, even though it is designed for those who already have solidity experience, I think this is still appropriate with those who want to learn solana at the start. Spent about 20 days learning anchor and native solana, pda cpi all kinds of that stuff, and wrote some small smart contract with anchor. Then I read some articles of common solana vulnerabilities and did the Neodyme ctf although it is very old, also read the SPL program. After that I decided to learn with a real program. I chose MetaDao since it is quite a complicated project with several concepts like AMM, Dao governance and prediction market. The hardest part for me is the internal arbitrage after swap and multiple outcomes of prediction market, which MetaDao is binary. I spent a long time understanding the code. During this process I also found an issue which was confirmed later in the audit report, there is no upper limit of outcomes. I was thinking about what if there are a lot of outcomes that may stuck the program. This is the very first issue I smelled and gives me positive feedback. Later I read the audit report and understood every finding. This also made me realize how hard a real audit is. It is not like a ctf which you know there is an issue, also not like a contest which it is okay to miss some findings. I was still wondering how to confirm the completion of an audit.

Then I spent a week building a Solana audit agent with python, 8 agents each in a different root cause. Then findings pass a judge and a dedup agent. Also a poc agent which creates an environment for each individual project and an assertion agent write assertions, the execution agent write poc for each finding to pass the assertion, for finding validation. Also a report generate agent to make the output a formal report. After testing with different models and programs, it has the best performance with opus-5. I tried several methods to lower the false positive rates and spent most of the time on design and testing the dedup process.

Then I started to play the Solana Audit Arena, a real-audit like CTF. I found some bugs and missed some. For most bug I missed in ctf or shadow audit has a root cause reason which is I didn't fully understand the program and Solana.  I started to think about what to do next, I decided to dive deeper into the SVM. Reading some important components of program runtime and sbf-vm, and figure out what actually happened behind the smart contract layer. The compiler's behavior is also important. I also read almost all of the research blogs and papers from Ottersec and Zellic.

Currently, the hardest problem I can't solve is I miss the correct methodology for audit and I believe it will not be efficient to explore with myself. I was being skeptical and made a lot of assumptions when I read the code, and verified each of them. I feel like I was totally based on intuition. I decided to stop practicing the wrong way but understanding everything from the very bottom, which I believe will have better outcomes.

Based on my understanding, the methodology of learning a new blockchain is to start with the programming language syntax like solidity, then learn the smart contract and the stack, like anchor. Then look into some common vulnerabilities and read some real protocol code. Dive into the vm and low level component, understanding everything behind the execution of a smart contract code is more important than the experience layer. After that, do shadow audits and read real audit reports, combined with fuzzing or FV. Currently I'm doing the same thing with EVM, and trying to learn Formal Verification. I'm also participating in season 2 of Solana Audit Arena, I think this is the best form of ctf in Solana and other blockchain however, currently the winning is all about speed. I think it will be better to not disclose findings until the end of the contest, which focuses more on the recall and quality of findings. I will move with the next step after I finish all of these.
